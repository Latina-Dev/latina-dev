An employee resource group (ERG) is a group run by employees who share an identity or experience, backed by the company. If your company doesn't have one for Latina and Latino employees, you can start it. Here is the order that tends to work.

## 1. Find your first members

Start with three to five people who want this to exist. Post in a company-wide channel, ask in other ERGs, and talk to people you already know. A small group that shows up beats a long list of names.

## 2. Check how ERGs work at your company

Ask HR or the diversity and inclusion team whether there is a process for new ERGs. Many companies have an application, a minimum number of members and a set budget per group. If other ERGs exist, ask their leads what they wish they had known.

## 3. Find an executive sponsor

A sponsor is a senior leader who speaks up for the group, helps it get budget and clears blockers. They don't need to be Latina or Latino. They do need to care and to have time for a check-in every month or two.

## 4. Pick a name and write a mission

Many Latinx ERGs use a name in Spanish, like Somos, Unidos, Hola or Juntos. See [what other companies call theirs](/ergs). Then write one or two sentences on who the group is for and what it will do. Decide early whether allies can join. Most groups say yes.

## 5. Set a few goals you can measure

Pick two or three goals for the first six months, each with a number. For example: 25 members, one event a month, or a mentoring pair for every new hire who joins the group. Numbers make it much easier to ask for budget later.

## 6. Ask for a budget

Write down what you would spend on: food for events, speakers, swag, conference tickets, a donation to a community group. Tie each line to a goal. Ask your sponsor to help you bring it to whoever owns the money.

## 7. Agree on how you'll meet

Choose a regular time, keep an agenda, take notes and share them. Rotate who runs the meeting so the group doesn't depend on one person. Keep a channel where anyone can ask questions between meetings.

## 8. Hold a first event

Keep it simple: a lunch, a panel with Latina and Latino leaders at the company, or a Hispanic Heritage Month kickoff (September 15 to October 15). Invite the whole company and ask people to join the group on the way out.

## 9. Share what you did

Every quarter, send your sponsor and members a short update: members, events, what worked and what you need. This is also what keeps the work visible when it's time for your own performance review.

## Protect your time

Running an ERG is real work on top of your job. Share the load with co-leads, ask for it to count in your reviews, and step back when you need to. There is more on this in [advice for the workplace](/guides/workplace-advice).
