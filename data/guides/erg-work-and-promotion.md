An employee resource group (ERG) is a group run by employees who share an identity or experience, backed by the company. Joining one is usually worth it. Leading one is a bigger question, because it is often work that helps the company and does little for your own promotion.

## The problem: non-promotable work

Researchers call tasks that help the organization but don't count in reviews "non-promotable work". Studies by Linda Babcock and her co-authors, written up in the book _The No Club_, found that women are asked to do this kind of work more often than men and say yes more often. Running an ERG, planning the heritage month event and sitting on the diversity committee usually fall into this bucket.

The time is real. Every hour spent booking a speaker is an hour not spent on the project your promotion case depends on.

## Join for what it gives you

Being a member costs little and can pay off:

- People who share your background and know how the company really works
- Senior leaders you would not otherwise meet, including possible sponsors
- A place to ask about pay, levels and managers
- Early word on open roles and teams that are good to work on

You can get all of this without holding a title in the group. See [which companies have a Latinx ERG](/resources/ergs).

## Before you say yes to leading

Ask these first, and get the answers in writing:

1. **Will it count in my review?** Ask your manager to add it to your goals and to say how it will be weighed. If the answer is "it's extra", treat it as extra.
2. **Is it paid or recognized?** Some companies pay ERG leads a stipend or bonus, or grant equity. Ask HR what yours does.
3. **How much time, and what comes off my plate?** Agree on hours per week and what work you will drop to make room.
4. **Who is the executive sponsor?** A sponsor who will speak for you at promotion time is the best thing a lead role can give you. No sponsor, no deal.
5. **How long is the term?** Pick an end date, like one year, and a co-lead to share the load.

## Make it promotable

If you do lead, shape the role so it shows the skills your next level asks for:

- Choose work with business results: hiring, retention, product feedback from Latino customers. Measure it.
- Own a budget and report on it. That is leadership scope you can point to.
- Present results to executives yourself. Visibility is the point.
- Write it into your promotion packet in the language of your level guide: scope, impact, influence.
- Hand off the logistics. Booking rooms and ordering food should rotate, or go to a paid coordinator.

## It's fine to say no

You can care about the community and still decline. "I can't take that on this half, I'm focused on my promotion" is a full answer. Suggest someone who would benefit from the visibility, or offer one small, bounded thing instead. There is more on protecting your time in [advice for the workplace](/guides/workplace-advice).
